Who it is for

Anyone whose question has a where, a what and a when: a PhD on irrigation law and olive yields, a report on heat and hospital admissions by region, a history of a river basin's dams. You do not need to code to see your first field; you need Node on a laptop to build the full plate — one command, printed for you.

The three questions

1 · Place

Where?

One country or several — the mask. The frame draws land, coast, relief and every river from open sources; no political line is drawn. The window is the mask's bounding box plus a margin.

2 · Data

What?

Layers of four shapes: a field (values on a grid — rain, heat), marks (values on places — regions, sites), events (dated instruments with a scope — laws, plans, dams), series (national figures — exports, prices). Readers exist for NASA POWER (worldwide) and Eurostat (EU); anything else comes in as CSV in the same shape.

3 · Years

When?

A start, an end, and a normal period for anomalies (WMO 1991–2020 by default). Every layer is read on that one axis, so a year is the same year on the sheet, in the margin and on the strip.
